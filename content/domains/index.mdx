# Foundations

The Ask-Then-Check Loop draws on six bodies of knowledge. Each one has its own section here, written for a general reader, with practitioner depth marked where it appears.

## Why these areas

Every step of the loop needs facts and judgment from several fields at once: how the tool works, what the law says, how a number is built, how a fraud is run. Grouping the material into areas lets you find one topic without reading the whole guide, and keeps each fact in one place.

## The areas

- [How language models answer](/domains/how-language-models-answer): What the tool is doing when it replies, why it can state a wrong fact with confidence, and why it tends to agree with you.
- [Privacy and your data](/domains/privacy-and-your-data): What happens to what you type, what never to paste, and how to ask a question without handing over your accounts.
- [Primary sources](/domains/primary-sources): Where money rules actually live, how to read a statute, agency page, or form, and how to tell whether a source is current.
- [Numbers and assumptions](/domains/numbers-and-assumptions): Where the arithmetic goes wrong, the assumptions hidden inside a projection, and quick checks that catch both.
- [Scams and impersonation](/domains/scams-and-impersonation): How the same tools are used against you: cloned voices, fake advisers, fake documents, and manufactured urgency.
- [Information versus advice](/domains/information-versus-advice): What an answer from a tool is and is not, when a decision needs a licensed person, and what to ask them.

<Callout type="info">
This guide is educational material about using AI assistants on your own money. It is not financial, tax, legal, or investment advice, and it does not create an advisory or professional relationship. Rules vary by country and by state, and both the rules and the tools change over time. Verify anything here against a current primary source, and get your own professional advice before acting.
</Callout>
