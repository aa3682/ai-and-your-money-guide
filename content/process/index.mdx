# The Ask-Then-Check Loop

Using an AI assistant on your own money goes better as a repeatable sequence than as a string of one-off questions. This section walks through that sequence step by step, with one page for each.

## Why a sequence

A question typed on impulse skips the two things that protect you: deciding whether the tool is right for this question at all, and checking the answer before you act on it. A fixed order makes sure neither is skipped, and each step produces exactly what the next one needs.

## The steps

1. [Decide what to use it for](/process/decide-what-to-use-it-for): Which money questions suit an AI assistant, and which never do.
2. [Understand how it answers](/process/understand-how-it-answers): What the tool is doing when it replies, and where that goes wrong.
3. [Choose what you share](/process/choose-what-you-share): What to give it, what to hold back, and how to strip out what it does not need.
4. [Ask it well](/process/ask-it-well): How to frame the question so the answer is usable and its limits are visible.
5. [Check what it tells you](/process/check-what-it-tells-you): How to test a figure, a rule, or a recommendation against a source you can trust.
6. [Act, record and review](/process/act-record-and-review): How to act on a checked answer, keep a note of what you relied on, and know when to hand the question to a person.

## A loop, not a line

The last step feeds the first. Acting on an answer and reviewing how it turned out tells you which kinds of question the tool handled well and which it did not, and that goes straight back into deciding what to use it for next time. Each pass through the loop should make the next one shorter.
