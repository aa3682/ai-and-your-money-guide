# Glossary

This page defines every term the guide links to, in plain English and for education rather than advice. Any number a term depends on, such as a limit, rate, or age, lives on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures) rather than here.

### Anonymise [#anonymise]

To remove or replace the details in a question or document that would identify you or your accounts, keeping only what the answer turns on. Names become roles, account numbers are dropped, and exact figures become round ones where exactness does not matter.

### Hallucination [#hallucination]

A confident, fluent statement from an AI assistant that is not true: a rule that does not exist, a figure that was never published, a source nobody wrote. It reads the same as a correct answer and can only be caught by checking outside the tool.

### Language model [#language-model]

The system behind an AI assistant: software trained on a very large body of text to predict what word plausibly comes next. It composes replies rather than looking them up, which is why it is fluent, confident, and sometimes wrong.

### Leading question [#leading-question]

A question whose wording signals the answer the asker expects, such as "why is this a bad idea". An AI assistant tends to answer a leading question in the direction it points, so the reply reflects the question more than the facts.

### Personal data [#personal-data]

Any detail that identifies you or could be used to get into your accounts: your name, address, date of birth, identity and account numbers, credentials, and documents that carry them. The tool rarely needs any of it to answer a money question.

### Primary source [#primary-source]

The original place a fact comes from rather than a summary of it: the statute, the agency's own page or form, the lender's own agreement, or the research paper. For a money rule, it is the document a court or regulator would point to, not an article or an answer about it.

### Prompt [#prompt]

The text you type into an AI assistant: the question, any facts you include, and any instructions about how to answer. The reply is shaped by the prompt more than by anything else, which is why the fourth step of the loop is about writing it.

### Training cutoff [#training-cutoff]

The date up to which the text used to train a language model was collected. Rules, rates and products that changed after it are absent from the model or present in their old form, unless the tool has been given a way to look things up.
