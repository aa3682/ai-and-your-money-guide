# Introduction

This guide explains how to use an AI assistant on your own money without being misled, from deciding what to ask it to checking what it tells you before you act. It is free, public, and written in plain English.

## What this guide is

This is an open reference on using AI on your own money. It describes the sequence a careful use follows, the knowledge that sequence draws on, and one checklist that makes the checking concrete.

It is not a guide to budgeting, investing, or tax. Those subjects have their own guides; this one is about the tool in your hand and what it does to a money question when you ask one.

## Who it is for

The first audience is anyone who manages their own money and already asks an AI assistant about it, or is about to: what to do with a bonus, whether a fee is normal, how two loans compare, what a letter from a lender means. You need no background in how these tools work.

The second audience is the adviser or planner whose clients do this. Where a page has something to say to that reader, it sits in a clearly marked subsection, so both readers follow the same pages.

## How it is organized

- **[The Ask-Then-Check Loop](/process).** Six steps, in order, from deciding what the tool is for to acting on what it tells you.
- **[Foundations](/domains).** The six bodies of knowledge the steps draw on: how these tools answer, what happens to what you share, where money rules actually live, and more.
- **[Tools](/tools).** A checklist for checking an answer, and the one page that holds any figure set by law or regulation.
- **[Glossary](/glossary).** Plain definitions of the terms used throughout the guide.

## How to use it

If you are new to the subject, read [The Ask-Then-Check Loop](/process) in order. Each step builds on the one before it. If you have a specific question, go straight to the relevant [Foundation](/domains). Every area page stands on its own.
